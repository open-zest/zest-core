# Project ZeST — Zero-Sort Transport

> **Sequenced Loads Eliminate the Need for In-Process Sort.**

## Overview

Project ZeST (**Open ZeST**) is an open-source systems architecture and simulation framework for European intermodal freight.

The final destination address of a shipping container is known the moment the contract is let to ship it. Using that knowledge—and the ocean transit lead time that comes with it—container loads can be sequenced so a box moves from the outbound seaport yard to final delivery in Europe in **exactly three lifts**, without ever touching the ground at the inbound seaport:

1. **Lift 1 (Outbound Seaport):** Yard to vessel, pre-sequenced by European destination catchment zone.
2. **Lift 2 (Inbound Seaport):** Direct ship-to-rail transfer—eliminating port yard stacking and re-handles.
3. **Lift 3 (Inland Dry Port / Terminal):** Rail car to final-mile truck for customer delivery.

## Practical Reality in 2026

Zero-Sort Transport is not science fiction or a decade-long infrastructure wishlist. It is practical reality today:

* **No Massive CapEx Required:** Virtual port capacity is unlocked through pre-arrival synchronization rather than new quay walls.
* **Commercial Off-The-Shelf (COTS):** Every technical building block already exists in commercial use across maritime, rail, and manufacturing process control.
* **Regulatory Tailwinds:** Existing European Union mandates for **eBL** and **eFTI (EU 2020/1056)** create the standardized, harmonized digital plumbing needed to separate paperwork from physical iron ahead of July 2027.

Project ZeST lays out the open engineering roadmap for the next logical step in European intermodal freight—bringing the network under control first, balancing regional gateways, and giving shippers predictable, rail-cost delivery timing across the continent.
